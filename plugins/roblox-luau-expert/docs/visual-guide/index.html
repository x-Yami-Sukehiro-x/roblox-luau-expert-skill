    Toggles

    All twenty turn a setting on or off. Tap each to try it.

    Every row is at least 44 pixels tall so it is easy to hit on a phone, and each shows “on” with shape or words, not colour alone.

    Checkboxes

    For lists where several things can be on at once, or one of a few choices. Tap to try.

    Tick boxes T3 and T10 above work too; these ten add groups, “select all”, one-choice circles, chips and tiles.

    Dropdowns

    Picking one thing from a list, several things, or searching. Open each and choose.

    Lists open over everything else, flip upward near the bottom of the screen, and close on a tap outside or Escape.

    Menu movement

    How a window appears and goes away. Pick one for opening and one for closing — they can be different — then replay your pair in the first card.

    Most arrive in 0.2 seconds and leave in 0.15. Players with reduced motion get a short fade instead, whatever you pick.

    Notifications

    Where messages appear and how they arrive. Press Show example.

    All of them use the same colours and corners as your window, carry an icon, stay at least 1.5 seconds, and errors stay until closed.

    Hide & bring back

    What happens when the player closes or minimises the whole UI, and how they get it back. Press Replay, or use the demo's own buttons.

    Every choice leaves something on screen to bring the window back, because a keybind alone strands a phone player.

    Button feel

    What a button does under your finger or mouse. Hover and press each one.

    All of them change on hover, on press and on keyboard focus; the ones that grow or shrink never move the buttons around them.

    Tab switch

    How the chosen tab is shown when you switch pages. Tap the tabs.

    The chosen tab stays marked; hovering or keyboard focus never changes it.

    Your picks

    Paste this into your chat. You can also mix and describe: “T1 but square”, “pop in, fade out”, “like N4 but at the top”.

    Skipped a group? Your AI uses its default for it and tells you which one: T1, C1, D1 (D2 for long lists, D10 to pick a player), M1 to open and close, N1 (N4 for a script hub), O1, P1 and S1.

    Want to arrange a whole screen yourself? Use the UI designer; it copies a layout your AI can rebuild exactly.

They show the look and timing each code stands for; they are not Roblox screenshots, and the finished interface still gets tested in Roblox on a computer and a phone.
